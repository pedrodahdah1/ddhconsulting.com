The situation

The data is there. The system is not.

Most of the organizations we sit with already have a practice, pharmacy, or billing system, a general ledger, bank activity, and payroll. None of it becomes a monthly picture leadership can use until someone stitches it together. That stitching is the work.

Month-end

The close still depends on a workbook

The same exports are pulled, pasted, and repaired every month. One late file and the package slips.

In the retainer: scheduled pulls and a monthly package that does not wait on a manual rebuild.
Cash

Deposits, remittances, and the ledger disagree

Cash hits the bank, payers send remittances, and the general ledger shows something else. The difference gets plugged.

In the retainer: a tie-out across those layers, with each exception listed and a reason beside it.
Incentives

Compensation is rebuilt every cycle

Provider incentives or sales commissions are reconstructed by hand, so every payout raises a question about the number.

In the retainer: a documented formula, a calculation that can be rerun, and a statement each person can check.
Visibility

A direct question takes days

Which site is off, which payer is slow, which provider is an outlier. The data exists. The answer is still another spreadsheet.

In the retainer: dashboards by location, payer, provider, or service line, on a schedule.
Key person

One person knows how reporting works

The process lives in one workbook and one memory. When that person is out, the month stops.

In the retainer: a written process the client's own team can follow.
Legacy BI

The retainer

What an embedded partner does, month to month.

DDH stays with finance and operations after the first build. One principal is accountable for the reporting, the close, the cash picture, the calculations in scope, and the models those numbers come from. You meet that same person at the leadership review.

Each month

  • Management reporting by location, provider, payer, or service line, refreshed on a schedule
  • Close support so the package is produced without rebuilding workbooks by hand
  • Cash and a forecast updated as deposits and remittances land
  • Commission and incentive calculations rerun from a documented formula
  • Reconciliation review across the bank, remittances, and the general ledger, with exceptions explained
  • A leadership review of what moved, why, and what needs a decision
  • Analysis when it cannot wait for a payer, a site, a provider, or a contract question
  • Steady improvement of pipelines, definitions, and models, including AI-assisted automation where it removes manual preparation

A small number of retainers. The same principal.

The engagement is scoped after a Data & Reporting Diagnostic and written down before onboarding. Scope follows what the organization actually needs.

  • Healthcare finance judgment and the system that produces the number
  • Claims, revenue-cycle, and provider data, used with that judgment
  • Work built in your environment
  • You own the data and the code
  • A Business Associate Agreement when protected health information is in scope

Focus areas

Four workstreams, one engagement.

These are the kinds of work a retainer includes. They are weighted to what the Diagnostic finds. They are not a menu of separate packages.

01
Close and reporting automation

Month-End Autopilot

Scheduled extracts, reconciliations between the operating system, remittances, and the general ledger, and a monthly package that goes out without a workbook rebuild. Differences are listed with a reason.

Inside the retainerA close that keeps its date, and a package more than one person can produce.

02
Cash and forecasting

Cash Truth

What has actually been collected, what is still outstanding by payer, and a forecast finance can update as deposits and remittances arrive. Built for conversations about liquidity and payer speed.

Inside the retainerA cash picture that is ready between meetings, maintained as the month moves.

03
Commission and incentive calculations

Commission Engine

Provider compensation and sales commissions run from a documented formula. The same logic produces each cycle, with a statement the person being paid can review.

Inside the retainerA calculation that can be explained, cycle after cycle.

04
SQL Server, SSAS, Power BI, Fabric

BI Modernization

Legacy SQL Server and SSAS reporting moved onto Power BI or Microsoft Fabric. Pipelines from claims and revenue-cycle data, practice or pharmacy systems, accounting, payroll, and the bank. Public sources, including CMS and NPPES, where they strengthen a provider or market view. AI-assisted automation only where it removes repetitive preparation, with a person still accountable for the figure leadership sees.

Inside the retainerOne governed model, documented, in the client's environment.

How it works

From a conversation to a monthly cadence.

The Data & Reporting Diagnostic is the first step of the work. A written proposal follows it. The retainer starts after that proposal is agreed.

Step 01
Intro call
A working conversation

With finance or operations leadership. How the close, the cash, the reporting, and the incentives work today, and which questions still go unanswered.

Step 02
Diagnostic
The first step

A Data & Reporting Diagnostic. We review source systems, definitions, and the close, and what leadership cannot see yet. You receive a written view of the gaps and a recommended scope.

Step 03
Written proposal
Before any build

Workstreams, cadence, and how the retainer will run, agreed before onboarding begins.

Step 04
Onboarding
In your environment

Access, a Business Associate Agreement when protected health information is in scope, pipelines and models, and a first package tested against closed periods.

Step 05
Monthly cadence
Leadership reviews

Reporting on a set rhythm, a review with leadership, and continued work on the systems underneath.

Trust

How data and ownership are handled.

HIPAA-aware handling

Financial reporting is designed so it does not need patient-level detail unless the question truly requires it. What is shared is limited to what the work needs. The contact form is not a place to send clinical information.

A BAA is available

When protected health information is in scope, a Business Associate Agreement is signed before any data is shared.

You own the data and the code

Systems are built in your environment and documented so your team, or someone you hire later, can run them. The client owns its data and its code.

Principal

You work with the person doing the work.

DDH Consulting is led by Pedro Dahdah. He is the principal on every retainer: the healthcare finance questions, and the systems that answer them. That includes the models, the pipelines, the reconciliations, and the reporting leadership uses.

A finance lead who cannot build leaves a list of wishes. A developer who does not know healthcare finance builds reports that do not tie to the bank, the remittance, or the general ledger. The retainer is accountable to both.

Areas of practice
  • Healthcare finance. Close, cash, forecasting, and incentive calculations.
  • Claims and revenue-cycle data. What was billed, what was collected, and where the two disagree.
  • Provider data. Including public CMS and NPPES datasets, where they sharpen a provider or market view.
  • The stack the reporting sits on. SQL Server, SSIS, SSAS, Power BI, and Microsoft Fabric.
  • Pipelines and automation. Including AI-assisted preparation, with a person accountable for the number.
